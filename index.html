Introduction

Systems utilizing Machine Learning models are increasingly deployed across domains involving high-stakes decisions, where they replace, improve, or assist decisions traditionally made by humans. However, their predictions often rely on internal representations, weights, embeddings, and learned feature interactions that have no natural correspondence to human reasoning. Any decision-making system, not only those built on machine learning, faces an explainability problem when its decisions are used to justify consequential outcomes. Explainability is the property by which a model can produce details or reasons that make its functioning easy and clear to understand

Symbolic rule-based classifiers mitigate the problem of explainability by construction rather than reconstruction. They consist of explicit “IF-THEN” decision rules over meaningful features. In this project, we study Exception Closed Conjuctive Rule Sets, (ECCRS); it is built for rules learnt from data with exceptions and conflict, resolving them through a Most Specific Wins(MSW) operator. MSW states that when multiple rules apply from a given instance ("situation"), the rule which is strictly more specfic than the other determines the conclusion / prediction. The explainability of this classifier comes naturally as 1) A set of rules that applies in a given instance, 2) Rules that are strictly more specific than other, and overrides them 3) The prediction/conclusion from the surving rules.

ECCRS' inference method for dealing with exception and conclusion is clean and straighforward , producing human readable explanation. It does not, however establish that the resulting decision is semantically correct under a formal theory of reasoning with exceptions. This motivates checking ECCRS against such a theory: the KLM framework, a standard formal account of defeasible reasoning, where conclusions hold by default but may be withdrawn when more specific information applies, developed by Kraus, Lehmann and Magidor \cite{Kraus90}. The framework offers several concrete methods for deciding what a set of default rules implies; we use the following ones: Rational Closure, Lexicographic Closure and System W.

  • Translate ECCRS rule sets into KLM knowledge bases.
  • Implement Rational Closure, Lexicographic Closure and System W.
  • Compare every prediction against the classifier, instance by instance.

Background

ECCRS

Classifiers make high-stakes calls such as loans and diagnoses, and people need to know why. Rule-based classifiers learn IF-THEN rules from data, so every prediction comes with the rule that made it. When rules conflict, ECCRS lets the most specific rule win (MSW).

Defeasible reasoning

Defeasible reasoning is the formal logic of exceptions: birds typically fly, penguins are birds, penguins don't fly. Madzime and Meyer proved that, under three structural assumptions, MSW gives the same answers as KLM defeasible entailment. We test that claim on real rule sets.

The workbench

A Python/C workbench checks the three assumptions, runs MSW inference with an explanation trace for every instance, translates the rule set into a KLM knowledge base, and asks each closure whether the instance entails m or ¬m. The two sides are then compared side by side.
